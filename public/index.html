Liver cancer research, in plain language

Straight answers about liver cancer, drawn from published medical research — so you can walk into an appointment knowing what to ask.

Hepatica explains research. It cannot tell you whether you have liver cancer.
No tool can do that without an exam, imaging, and lab work. If something is worrying you, see a healthcare provider. For jaundice, vomiting blood, black stools, confusion, or severe abdominal pain, seek care right away.
Privacy: this site runs on Google's free AI tier, so what you type may be used by Google to improve their models. Leave out your name and contact details, and ask in general terms where you can.
Somewhere to start

Research is pulled live from PubMed (National Library of Medicine) and ClinicalTrials.gov each time you ask. Answers are written by Google Gemini and can be wrong — check anything that matters against the linked source or with a clinician.

Nothing is sent to or stored on this server. Your conversation is saved only in this browser, on this device, so you can come back to it — clear it any time with the button at the top. The site runs on free infrastructure, so there is a limit on how many questions it can answer each day.

Population statistics come from the NCI SEER Program and the American Cancer Society. Survival figures describe large groups of people diagnosed years ago and cannot predict any individual outcome.

Named for Hepatica, a woodland flower that blooms before anything else in spring. Its leaves were once thought to resemble the liver.
